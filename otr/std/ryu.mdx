The shortest decimal that reads back as a given float, by Ryu (Ulf Adams, "Ryū: fast
float-to-string conversion", PLDI 2018), which `std.io` writes floats with. It needs
no allocation and no big integers: two 64x64-bit multiplications by a precomputed
power of five give the bounds of the rounding interval, and digits are dropped until
they would leave it.

```otr
import std.ryu
```

## Shortest

```otr
const Shortest = struct
```

A float's shortest decimal: `digits` times ten to the `exp`, with the fewest digits
that read back as the float, and the one nearest to it among those.

| Field | Type |
| --- | --- |
| <code>digits</code> | <code>u64</code> |
| <code>exp</code> | <code>i32</code> |

## shortest

```otr
fn shortest(mantissa: u64, exponent: u32, comptime mbits: u32, comptime bias: i32) Shortest
```

The shortest decimal for a finite, nonzero float of a binary format with `mbits`
mantissa bits and exponent `bias` (52 and 1023 for `f64`, 23 and 127 for `f32`, 10
and 15 for `f16`), given its raw mantissa and exponent fields. The tables are those of
`f64` and serve the narrower formats too, whose mantissas are shorter.
